Four ways to squint

Four directions for the macOS app icon, each shown on a black and a white desktop, then stepped down to the sizes that actually decide it. The Finder context menu renders at 16 px. A concept that dissolves in the last column cannot ship, however well it reads large.

A

Slit

The name, drawn.

An eye narrowed to a lens with a pupil still visible. The most direct reading of the word, and the only concept a stranger decodes without being told the app's name.

B

Aperture

Photographic, not ocular.

Six iris blades closing down. It borrows authority from camera hardware and signals image work rather than vision — but aperture marks are common in photo apps, so it is the least distinctive.

C

Twin

The thesis as a joke.

Two identical squares. One is a hairline, one is solid. Same shape, different weight — which is the entire product argument. It needs a beat to land, and never lands at 16px.

D

Lash

Reduction to a gesture.

The lid line alone. Closest to the hairline discipline in your brand system, where structure is drawn with rules and never filled boxes. Also the most fragile when it shrinks.

Recommendation: A, Slit. It is the only one that survives the 16 px column with its meaning intact, and the only one that explains the app's name to someone encountering it cold. B is competent but generic in a category full of apertures. C is the smartest idea here and the worst icon — the joke needs two shapes and a second of attention, neither of which a menu row grants. D is the most native to your brand system and the first to disappear.
